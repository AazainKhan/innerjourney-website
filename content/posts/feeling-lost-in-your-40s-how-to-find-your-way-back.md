**Feeling Lost in Your 40s? How to Find Your Way Back**

Someone asks you a simple question at dinner. "So what do you enjoy doing?"

And you go blank.

You could tell them what your team needs by Friday. What your family needs this weekend. What your parents need. But what do you enjoy? You have to stop and think. The longer you think, the stranger it feels.

If you are feeling lost in your 40s, it often shows up in small moments like this. Nothing dramatic. Just a quiet knowing that you've been running on autopilot. At times you wonder whose plan you are following.

In this post I'll share why this feeling arrives in your 40s, why it can be most confusing when life looks perfect, and small ways to find your way back.

**Why feeling lost in your 40s is so common**

You are far from the only one. Economist David Blanchflower studied how happy people feel in more than 140 countries. When he drew it on a graph by age, the line made a U shape. It drops through the middle years and rises again later. So if you feel lost right now, you may simply be in the dip. For most people, the line climbs back up.

**Your days are full, but not always yours**

By your 40s, most of your time is spoken for. Meetings, deadlines, family, friends. The endless small decisions that keep everything moving. Each part makes sense on its own. Together, they leave very little room for you.

You don't lose yourself all at once. You lose yourself in small pieces, one busy week at a time.

**You finally have a moment to look up**

For years there was no space to ask big questions. Then something eases. The children, if you have them, need you less. A big project ends. A routine you've kept for years loosens.

In that new space, the questions arrive. Is this it? Is this what I want?

It can feel uncomfortable. But often it means you finally have the time and space to hear yourself.

**The old map no longer matches the road now**

Many women are still following plans they made at 25. Those plans once gave direction. The road has changed, but the map hasn't. So you keep walking, and nothing looks quite familiar.

Social media doesn't help. Everyone else seems so sure of where they're going. But you're only seeing their best moments.

The woman who made those plans didn't know what you know now. It's fair to let your plans grow up with you.

**When life looks perfect and you still feel lost**

This is the part many women find hardest to say out loud.

You have the home.  You are placed in a desired career position. The holidays. The comfortable life you once hoped for. From the outside, nothing is missing.

So when the lost feeling arrives, it brings guilt with it. What right do I have to feel this way? Other people have real problems.

A client once sat across from me and said it simply. "Everything is perfect. So why do I feel like this?" “Am I being ungrateful?”

She couldn't find a reason. That worried her more than the feeling itself. She kept looking for something that had gone wrong. There was nothing.

As we worked together, she began to see it differently. For the first time in years, nothing needed fixing. With no problems left to solve, the quieter questions about her own life finally had room to come forward.

This is what I believe. Feeling lost in a comfortable life doesn't mean you're ungrateful. Often it means you finally have enough stillness to notice yourself. The comfort didn't create the emptiness. It simply stopped covering it.

**How to find your way back**

Finding yourself again rarely happens in one big breakthrough. It comes to the surface  small clues you start to notice.

**Pay attention to envy**

Envy has a bad reputation. But, trust me;  it can be an honest guide sometime.

Notice who you feel a little envious of. The friend who went back to study? The colleague who took a year out to travel? The cousin who started a book club? The friend who started a business of cooked meal from home?

Envy often points to something you want for yourself. You don't have to copy anyone. Just ask what it is about their life that tugs at you.

**Notice where time disappears**

Think of the last time an hour passed without you noticing. What were you doing?

It might be something small. Helping a friend think through a problem. Trying a new recipe. Getting lost in a book. These moments hold clues about what gives you energy.

**Visit the woman you were before**

This is the part, I love the most. Before you were someone's manager, partner or mother, you were someone. Ask yourself a few questions about **HER**.

•	What did she love to read?

•	What made her laugh until she cried?

•	What did her friends say about her?

•	What did she say she'd do one day?

Call a friend who knew you back then. Ask what they remember about you. You may be surprised.

**You can experiment rather than decide**

When you feel lost, it's tempting to wait until you know exactly what you want. That day rarely comes on its own. Step up. No more waiting.

Try small experiments instead. Take a one-day course. Go to an event alone. Say yes to something you'd usually turn down. Some will fall flat. That's a useful piece of information.  Each one shows you a little more about what fits.

**A question to sit with, and a challenge**

Here's a question for this week. If no one needed anything from you for a whole day, how would you spend it?

And here's the challenge. Pick one clue from this post. Just one. The envy that stung a little. The hour that disappeared. The thing you said you'd do one day.

Act on it this month. Before it feels clear. Before you feel ready.

I know that may sound heavy. When you feel lost, a small step can feel pointless. What's the use if I don't know where I'm going? And that doubt and unwillingness is okay to hear but to sit with it.  Clarity rarely comes first. It follows the step.

Some fear may show up too. That's normal. I've written a blog on fear and relationship with it.  You will find it helpful to unravel the approach.

**The good news about your 40s**

You arrive here with something you didn't have in your 20s. You know how to get through hard things. You know what matters to you and what doesn't. That knowledge helps you choose well now.

Feeling lost in your 40s can be unsettling. It can also be the moment you stop living by old plans and start choosing on purpose.
